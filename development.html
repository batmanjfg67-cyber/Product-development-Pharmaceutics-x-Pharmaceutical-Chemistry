01 / Formulation Design

The product we are developing.

Our proposed product is a 100 mg/mL pyrazinamide (PZA) oral suspension designed for paediatric tuberculosis treatment. The formulation uses a defined excipient system to address suspension behaviour, palatability, wetting, preservation and pH control.

Formulation Architecture

Every ingredient has a job.

The proposed formulation combines the active pharmaceutical ingredient with excipients selected to address the key requirements of the suspension.

Pyrazinamide

Active pharmaceutical ingredient providing antitubercular activity.

NaCMC

Suspending agent that supports viscosity, suspension stability and redispersibility.

Polysorbate 80

Wetting agent supporting dispersion of PZA particles throughout the vehicle.

Sucrose

Sweetener included as part of the palatability strategy.

Potassium Sorbate

Preservative included as part of the microbial protection strategy.

Citrate Buffer

Citric acid and sodium citrate provide pH adjustment and buffering.

Flavour

Included as part of the taste-masking and acceptability strategy.

Purified Water

Forms the continuous aqueous phase of the suspension.

03 / Manufacturing

Building the suspension.

The order of addition is designed to support proper hydration, wetting, dispersion and uniformity of the final suspension.

01

Prepare the workspace

Clean and sanitise the manufacturing area and equipment before beginning the formulation process.

02

Hydrate the NaCMC

Disperse the calculated sodium carboxymethylcellulose (NaCMC) into a portion of purified water and allow adequate hydration.

03

Prepare the aqueous phase

Prepare the vehicle containing sucrose, potassium sorbate and the citrate buffer components.

04

Wet the PZA

Accurately weigh the pyrazinamide (PZA). Wet the powder using a small amount of wetting-agent-containing vehicle until a smooth paste is formed.

05

Geometric dilution

Gradually incorporate the vehicle into the PZA paste using geometric dilution, mixing thoroughly after each addition.

06

Make up to volume

Transfer to a calibrated vessel and add purified water q.s. to 100 mL. Add the selected flavour at its predetermined concentration and mix thoroughly.

07

Fill and package

Transfer the finished suspension into a suitable light-resistant multidose bottle and supply a calibrated oral syringe.

08

Quality control

The finished formulation proceeds to physical, chemical and microbiological quality assessments.

04 / Suspension Science

What happens inside the bottle?

PZA is dispersed as solid particles rather than being completely dissolved in the vehicle. During storage, gravity can cause particles to settle.

This makes redispersibility important. The suspension should be capable of being adequately redistributed before administration.

The formulation therefore uses a suspending agent and wetting agent as part of the strategy for controlling suspension behaviour.

01 — Sedimentation
Particles settle during storage.
02 — Shake
The bottle is shaken.
03 — Redisperse
More uniform distribution for dosing.

05 / Packaging

Designed for use beyond the laboratory.

Packaging and administration are part of formulation development because the product must remain protected while allowing accurate paediatric dosing.

Light-resistant container

An amber or otherwise suitable light-resistant multidose bottle is proposed.

Calibrated oral syringe

Supports accurate measurement of the required liquid volume.

Clear instructions

The proposed label includes "Shake well before use", oral-use instructions and light protection.

Storage based on evidence

Final storage conditions and beyond-use period must be established through formulation-specific studies.
