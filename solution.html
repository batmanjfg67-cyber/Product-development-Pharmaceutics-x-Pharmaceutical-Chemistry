01 / DOSAGE FORM

Why an Oral Suspension?

An oral suspension is a liquid dosage form in which finely divided, insoluble solid particles are dispersed throughout a liquid vehicle.

A liquid designed for paediatric administration.

Young children may experience difficulty swallowing conventional tablets or capsules. A liquid dosage form can therefore provide a more practical method of administration.

Suspension technology is also particularly useful when an active pharmaceutical ingredient (API) has limited solubility in the selected vehicle. Instead of requiring the drug to remain completely dissolved, the API can be maintained as dispersed solid particles throughout the liquid.

The key idea: keep the drug as a liquid dosage form without requiring complete dissolution of the API.
KEY ADVANTAGES
Easier to Swallow

A liquid can be easier for young children to swallow than conventional solid dosage forms.

Flexible Dosing

The administered dose can be adjusted through the measured volume.

Suitable for Poorly Soluble APIs

Insoluble drug particles can be dispersed throughout the vehicle.

Palatability Considerations

A liquid formulation provides opportunities for sweetening and flavouring strategies.

THE TRADE-OFF

A Better Dosage Form Still Has Challenges.

Selecting a suspension solves some paediatric administration problems, but introduces formulation and dosing considerations that must be controlled during development.

Sedimentation

Solid particles may settle during storage.

Redispersion

The suspension needs adequate shaking before administration.

Dose Accuracy

Incorrect measurement or inadequate shaking may affect the administered dose.

Physical Stability

The formulation must remain adequately stable and readily redispersible.

02 / ACTIVE PHARMACEUTICAL INGREDIENT

Why Pyrazinamide?

Pyrazinamide (PZA) is an important first-line antituberculosis medicine and has a distinctive role in tuberculosis treatment because of its activity against persistent populations of Mycobacterium tuberculosis.

Pyrazinamide is administered as a prodrug and is converted within mycobacteria to its active metabolite, pyrazinoic acid (POA). This contributes to its activity against persistent bacilli and its role in shortening tuberculosis treatment.

03 / ROUTE OF ADMINISTRATION

Why the Oral Route?

The suspension is administered through the mouth and swallowed, providing a non-invasive route that is practical for repeated administration by children and caregivers.

Non-Invasive

Oral administration avoids invasive routes when an effective oral formulation is available.

Practical

Oral medicines can generally be administered outside a healthcare facility.

Suitable for Repeated Dosing

This is particularly relevant for TB treatment, which requires repeated administration.

Caregiver-Friendly

A liquid dosage form can support administration by caregivers when appropriate instructions and measuring devices are provided.

The oral route is not without limitations.

Vomiting, difficulty swallowing, refusal to take medication, unpleasant taste and inaccurate measurement can interfere with administration.
