FROM FORMULATION TO PATIENT

The Impact

A paediatric oral suspension can address practical challenges associated with administering tuberculosis treatment to children by making dosing, administration and medicine use more accessible.

WHY IT MATTERS

Turning pharmaceutical development into patient benefit

The value of the formulation is not only in producing a stable medicine, but in addressing practical barriers that can affect how easily a child can receive treatment.

01

Easier administration

A liquid dosage form may be easier for children to swallow than conventional solid dosage forms, particularly when swallowing tablets or capsules is difficult.

02

Flexible dosing

An oral suspension allows the administered volume to be adjusted according to the required dose, supporting weight-based paediatric dosing as the child grows.

03

Improved acceptability

Sweeteners and flavouring strategies can be incorporated to improve palatability and make repeated administration more acceptable to children.

PATIENT IMPACT

Designed around the needs of paediatric patients

Paediatric treatment requires more than simply reducing the strength of an adult dosage form. Administration, dosing accuracy and acceptability must all be considered.

PAEDIATRIC ADVANTAGE

Making treatment easier to use

The proposed suspension provides a liquid formulation that can be administered using a calibrated oral syringe. This can support accurate measurement of the required volume and avoid reliance on household spoons.

The formulation can also be designed with appropriate taste-masking and flavouring strategies to improve acceptability during prolonged treatment.

Accurate measured dosing
TREATMENT ACCEPTABILITY

Supporting treatment adherence

Paediatric tuberculosis treatment can involve prolonged therapy, making the acceptability and practicality of each medicine important considerations.

A better dosage form can remove practical barriers

Easier swallowing, flexible dosing and improved palatability may make administration more manageable for children and caregivers.

However, a more suitable dosage form does not automatically guarantee adherence. Palatability, usability and patient acceptability would need to be evaluated in the intended paediatric population.

QUALITY & SAFETY

Patient impact depends on product quality

A child-friendly dosage form must also deliver consistent and reliable doses throughout its intended storage period.

Drug content

Analytical testing can confirm that the formulation contains the intended amount of pyrazinamide.

Analytical reliability

A validated analytical method supports reliable identification and quantification of the active pharmaceutical ingredient.

Stability

Stability studies are required to evaluate changes in drug content, physical properties and other relevant quality attributes over time.

Microbial protection

Because the product is an aqueous formulation, appropriate preservation and microbiological evaluation are important considerations.

LIMITATIONS

What still needs to be established

The proposed formulation represents a pharmaceutical development concept and requires further evaluation before routine clinical use.

01

Stability and shelf life

Appropriate stability studies are required to establish the product's shelf life and suitable storage conditions.

02

Physical stability

Sedimentation, redispersibility, appearance and dose uniformity must remain acceptable throughout storage and use.

03

Palatability

Further taste-masking strategies and paediatric acceptability testing may be required to determine whether the formulation is sufficiently palatable.

04

Clinical evaluation

Additional pharmaceutical, microbiological and clinical evaluation would be required before the formulation could be considered for routine use.

FUTURE DEVELOPMENT

Where the project could go next

Further development could focus on improving the formulation while maintaining accurate dosing, stability and quality.

01

Palatability

Investigate improved flavouring and taste-masking approaches to increase acceptability.

02

Physical stability

Optimise the suspension system to maintain uniformity, suitable viscosity and redispersibility.

03

Packaging

Investigate packaging systems that support stability, storage and convenient administration.

04

Paediatric evaluation

Evaluate usability and acceptability in the intended paediatric population.

THE OVERALL IMPACT

Pharmaceutical development can make treatment more practical for children.

A well-designed paediatric oral suspension has the potential to address practical challenges associated with swallowing, dose measurement and medicine acceptability. However, meaningful patient benefit depends on demonstrating appropriate quality, stability, safety and acceptability through further testing.
