THE PROBLEM

Why Paediatric TB Treatment Needs a Child-Centred Approach.

Tuberculosis remains an important public health concern among children. Although major progress has been made in developing child-friendly TB medicines, challenges involving administration, palatability, dosing flexibility and adherence remain.

THE GLOBAL CONTEXT

Paediatric TB Remains a Significant Burden

Children and adolescents continue to represent an important proportion of the global tuberculosis burden, with younger children being particularly vulnerable to severe forms of disease.

10.7M estimated TB cases globally in 2024
1.2M children and young adolescents who developed TB in 2024
Younger children are at increased risk of severe forms of TB

CURRENT TREATMENT

Treatment Has Improved — But Challenges Remain.

Drug-susceptible paediatric TB is treated using combination therapy. Common medicines include rifampicin, isoniazid, pyrazinamide and ethambutol. Using multiple medicines is important for effective treatment and helps reduce the development of drug resistance.

Progress in Child-Friendly Treatment

Fixed-dose combinations can combine several anti-TB medicines into a single dosage form, reducing pill burden. Many paediatric formulations are dispersible tablets that can be mixed with water, making them more suitable for children who have difficulty swallowing conventional tablets.

TREATMENT DURATION 4 months

WHO recommends a four-month regimen for selected children with non-severe drug-susceptible TB. Children with severe disease may still require longer treatment.

THE REMAINING CHALLENGES

Where Do Difficulties Still Arise?

Even with improved child-friendly TB medicines, several pharmaceutical and practical factors can influence whether treatment can be administered accurately, comfortably and consistently.

01

Treatment Complexity

Children may require multiple medicines or dosage units depending on their weight and treatment regimen. This can increase the practical burden placed on both the child and caregiver.

02

Difficulty Swallowing

Younger children may not yet be able to swallow conventional tablets or capsules safely and comfortably. Dispersible formulations help address this issue, but appropriate liquid dosage forms remain relevant.

03

Palatability

Bitter or unpleasant tastes can cause children to refuse medicines. Even liquid medicines are not automatically palatable, making taste and mouthfeel important considerations for repeated administration.

04

Weight-Based Dosing

Paediatric doses are commonly determined using weight bands. As children grow, their weight and required doses can change, creating a need for accurate and flexible administration.

05

Repeated Administration

TB treatment requires medicines to be taken repeatedly over a sustained treatment period. This makes ease of administration and acceptability particularly important.

06

Adherence

Poor palatability, administration difficulties and complex regimens may contribute to poor adherence. Inconsistent treatment can reduce effectiveness and increase the risk of resistance.

07

Dosing Accuracy

Liquid dosage forms allow flexible dosing but introduce measurement considerations. An incorrect volume, inappropriate measuring device or inadequate redispersion can result in an inaccurate dose.

08

Formulation Stability

A suspension must remain physically stable throughout its intended shelf life. Sedimentation, caking, aggregation and changes in viscosity can affect uniformity and redispersion.

A CLOSER LOOK

Accurate Dosing Is More Than Just Choosing the Right Dose.

Paediatric TB treatment is closely linked to body weight. A formulation therefore needs to make the prescribed dose practical to measure and administer.

For an oral suspension, this means that the product must be adequately redispersed before use and supplied with an appropriate calibrated measuring device. Failure to shake the suspension adequately or using an unsuitable household spoon may result in the wrong amount of API being administered.

Flexible dosing must be supported by accurate measurement.

THE PAEDIATRIC PERSPECTIVE

Children Are Not Just "Small Adults."

Paediatric patients have unique physiological and practical needs. An appropriate dosage form should consider age, body size, swallowing ability, palatability, dosing requirements and the practical realities of administration by caregivers.

THE CAREGIVER MATTERS TOO

Treatment Happens Beyond the Clinic.

Paediatric medicines are often administered by parents or caregivers rather than independently by the child. The formulation therefore needs to be practical for everyday use outside a healthcare facility.

Clear instructions, appropriate measuring devices, correct redispersion, suitable storage and secure packaging all contribute to safe administration.
