THE ANALYTICAL QUESTION

How do we prove the product is correct?

The finished suspension must be converted into a suitable analytical sample before its pyrazinamide content can be measured.

Because the product is a suspension, representative sampling is essential. The sample must then undergo controlled preparation and dilution before being introduced into the chromatographic system.

SAMPLE DEFINITION

Start with a representative sample.

The analytical result is only meaningful if the portion removed from the finished product accurately represents the batch.

01

What is the sample?

A representative portion of the finished 100 mg/mL pyrazinamide oral suspension prepared for quantitative chemical analysis.

02

Why must it be representative?

PZA is dispersed as solid particles. Sedimentation can produce concentration differences within the container if the suspension is not adequately redistributed.

03

What must be controlled?

Sampling should control contamination, inaccurate volume transfer, incomplete transfer and inappropriate storage.

04 REFERENCE STANDARD

Build the measurement from a known standard.

A reference standard with known identity and purity provides the basis for comparing the analytical response of the unknown sample.

PZA REFERENCE STANDARD
STEP 01

Prepare stock solution

Weigh 100.0 mg of PZA reference standard and dilute to 100.0 mL with methanol.

100 mg ÷ 100 mL = 1.00 mg/mL
STEP 02

Prepare working standard

Transfer 1.00 mL of the 1.00 mg/mL stock solution to a 10.0 mL volumetric flask and dilute with methanol.

1.00 × 1.00 ÷ 10.0 = 0.100 mg/mL
STEP 03

Correct for standard purity

Where required, the certificate-of-analysis purity of the reference standard is incorporated into the concentration calculation.
